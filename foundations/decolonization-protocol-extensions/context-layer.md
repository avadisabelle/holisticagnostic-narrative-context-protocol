# Context Layer — NCP Decolonization: Protocol Extensions

## Canonical Terms

| Term | Definition in This Packet |
|------|--------------------------|
| **Data colonialism** | Couldry and Mejias's term for the extension of colonial power through the systematic appropriation of human life through data, treating social relations as raw material for extraction |
| **Data sovereignty** | The right of a nation, community, or people to govern the collection, ownership, and application of data about them — extended to Indigenous data sovereignty by Kukutai and Taylor |
| **Transmission conditions** | The set of requirements — teller, receiver, context, form, authorization — that must be satisfied for a story to travel ethically; context IS part of the protocol, not optional metadata |
| **Relational provenance** | The chain of relationships through which knowledge has traveled, including who authorized each transfer — distinct from document provenance, which tracks edits |
| **Linguistic frame** | The acknowledgment that a story's categories, concepts, and structures are shaped by the language in which it was first articulated, and that translation into another language (including JSON) introduces semantic loss |
| **Performativity** | Knowledge that exists only in enactment — dance, ceremony, song, embodied practice — and cannot be fully represented as text or structured data without fundamental distortion |
| **Semantic access control** | Access restrictions that operate at the level of meaning, not data — distinguishing what is transmissible, what is protocol-gated (accessible under conditions), and what is enacted (inseparable from its performance) |
| **Felicity conditions** | From speech act theory (Austin, Searle): the conditions that must be met for a speech act to be performed successfully — extended here to story transmission |
| **Extractivism** | The logic of treating knowledge, culture, or relationships as resources to be extracted, processed, and consumed — the colonial pattern that decolonial protocol design must resist |
| **TK Labels** | Traditional Knowledge Labels developed by Local Contexts — metadata markers communicating Indigenous expectations about access and use of digital content |
| **CARE Principles** | Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics — principles for Indigenous data governance developed by GIDA |
| **OCAP** | Ownership, Control, Access, Possession — First Nations information governance principles (FNIGC) |
| **Structural violence** | Harm embedded in systems and structures rather than enacted by individuals — relevant to how protocol design can perpetuate colonial patterns invisibly |

## Field Decomposition (MECE)

This packet decomposes into the following mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive fields:

1. **Decolonial critique** — analysis of Western assumptions embedded in the current NCP
2. **Transmission conditions layer** — felicity conditions for story delivery
3. **Relational provenance layer** — authorization chains and consent protocols
4. **Linguistic frame layer** — language-relativity acknowledgment
5. **Performativity layer** — enacted knowledge constraints
6. **Semantic access layer** — meaning-level access control
7. **Framework alignment** — mapping to CARE, OCAP, TK Labels, UNDRIP
8. **Anti-extraction safeguards** — mechanisms preventing digital colonialism
9. **Implementation considerations** — backward compatibility, optional/required fields

## Source Quality Rules

- **Prefer**: Decolonial computing scholarship, Indigenous data sovereignty literature, critical data studies
- **Prioritize**: Primary texts by Indigenous scholars and communities; peer-reviewed decolonial theory
- **Accept**: Strong practitioner writing from digital governance projects (Mukurtu, Local Contexts); international legal instruments (UNDRIP)
- **Avoid**: Secondary summaries without citation; "diversity" framing that centers Western institutions; commentary on Indigenous protocols without Indigenous authorship or collaboration
- **Note**: This packet discusses the *structure* of governance protocols without claiming to reproduce or represent specific Indigenous knowledge. The goal is to make the NCP *capable* of expressing conditions, not to populate those conditions on behalf of communities.
