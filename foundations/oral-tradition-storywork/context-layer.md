# Context Layer — Oral Tradition Studies & Storywork Methodology

## Canonical Terms

| Term | Definition in This Packet |
|------|--------------------------|
| **Oral tradition** | The systematic transmission of knowledge, stories, histories, and cultural values through spoken word across generations, operating as a technology with its own protocols for fidelity and access |
| **Storywork** | Jo-Ann Archibald's methodology for ethical engagement with Indigenous stories, governed by seven principles: respect, responsibility, reciprocity, reverence, holism, interrelatedness, synergy |
| **Psychodynamics of orality** | Walter Ong's term for the mental processes and cultural patterns characteristic of primarily oral societies |
| **Transmission** | The living, relational act of passing knowledge from teller to listener in context — distinct from archiving or recording |
| **Contextual protocol** | Rules governing when, where, by whom, and to whom a story may be told — context IS part of the message |
| **Story stewardship** | The responsibility of carrying, protecting, and correctly transmitting a story — distinct from ownership in the property sense |
| **Performance frame** | Bauman's concept: signals that mark an utterance as a special communicative event requiring audience evaluation |
| **Mnemonic technology** | Systematic use of rhythm, song, place, body, repetition, and formulaic expression to encode knowledge for reliable oral transmission |
| **Breakthrough into performance** | Hymes's concept: the moment when ordinary speech transforms into a heightened, artistic, socially recognized communicative event |
| **TK Labels** | Traditional Knowledge Labels — metadata markers developed by Local Contexts to communicate Indigenous expectations about access and use |

## Field Decomposition (MECE)

This packet decomposes into the following mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive fields:

1. **Storywork methodology** — Archibald's framework and its seven principles
2. **Orality studies** — Ong, Vansina, and the psychodynamics of oral knowledge systems
3. **Performance theory** — Bauman, Hymes, and the communicative event
4. **Mnemonic technologies** — encoding mechanisms in oral traditions
5. **Transmission protocols** — contextual, ethical, and relational rules of story movement
6. **Archiving vs. transmitting** — the critical distinction and its engineering implications
7. **Contemporary applications** — storywork in education, health, digital systems (Mukurtu)

## Source Quality Rules

- **Prefer**: Indigenous scholars, oral tradition specialists, ethnographic studies
- **Prioritize**: Primary texts (Archibald 2008, Ong 1982, Vansina 1985, Bauman 1977, Hymes 1975)
- **Accept**: Strong practitioner writing, documented applications, open-access Indigenous education resources
- **Avoid**: Secondary summaries without citation, undated web content, non-Indigenous commentary on Indigenous protocols without Indigenous co-authorship
- **Note**: Some knowledge about oral tradition protocols is itself governed by protocols — this packet discusses the *structure* of such protocols without claiming to reproduce restricted content
