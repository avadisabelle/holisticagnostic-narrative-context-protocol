## Why This Packet Exists

The Narrative Context Protocol (NCP) was forked into the Holistic Agnostic NCP repository with the intent to extend and decolonize the protocol — to make it capable of supporting narrative knowledge systems beyond the Western, English-derived, Dramatica-based framework it inherits.

This packet exists because that goal requires academic grounding. Without rigorous foundations in the relevant fields, protocol extensions risk:
- **Performative decolonization** — changing labels without changing power structures
- **Digital extraction** — encoding Indigenous protocols in ways that make them more extractable, not less
- **Category error** — treating non-Western narrative traditions as variants of Western ones, rather than as fundamentally different epistemic systems
- **Ungrounded claims** — proposing extensions without understanding the scholarly discourse that makes them legible

## Intended Audience

1. **Ava (primary)** — the steward of this fork, who identified the interlocking processes of linguistic encoding and knowledge validation as the foundation for decolonizing work
2. **NCP contributors** — who need to understand what the protocol's current categories assume and exclude
3. **Indigenous knowledge keepers** — who may evaluate whether protocol-based approaches can honor their traditions (this packet does not presume to answer that question)
4. **Protocol engineers** — who need concrete, implementable extension proposals grounded in scholarship

## Desired Outcome

After engaging with this packet, a reader should be able to:

1. **Name the academic fields** that ground the decolonization effort: linguistic anthropology, oral tradition studies, speech act theory, Indigenous research methodology, decolonial computing
2. **Articulate the structural tension** between the NCP's document-centric design and the performative, protocol-gated, relational nature of non-Western narrative knowledge
3. **Evaluate the proposed extensions** (transmission conditions, relational provenance, linguistic frame, performativity, semantic access) against both academic rigor and practical implementability
4. **Identify anti-extraction safeguards** that prevent the extensions from becoming tools for digital colonialism
5. **Understand the difference** between protocol *support* (expressing that conditions exist) and protocol *enforcement* (which remains a community function, not a technical one)

## Current Reality

The NCP currently:
- Encodes narrative as JSON objects with structured fields (perspectives, players, storypoints, storybeats, dynamics)
- Separates subtext (deep meaning) from storytelling (presentation)
- Assumes extractability — any serialized story can be parsed, copied, and transmitted
- Uses English-derived, Dramatica-based categories that embed Western narrative assumptions (individual protagonist, linear causality, conflict as engine)
- Has no apparatus for transmission conditions, receiver readiness, community authorization, or performativity
- Treats stories as documents, not as acts with conditions

## Structural Tension Being Resolved

The tension is between **what the NCP models** and **what narrative knowledge IS** in non-Western traditions:

| What NCP Models | What Non-Western Narrative Knowledge Is |
|----------------|----------------------------------------|
| Content (what the story says) | Act (what the story does) |
| Universal access by default | Protocol-gated access by default |
| Individual authorship | Community stewardship |
| Extractable data | Context-bound performance |
| Hierarchical containment | Relational web |
| Noun-centric objects | Verb-centric processes |
| Linear sequence | Cyclical, spiral, simultaneous temporality |
| Conflict as engine | Harmony, reciprocity, relational balance |

This packet does not resolve the tension. It makes the tension *visible* and *academically grounded* so that protocol extensions can address it with intellectual integrity.

## Decisions This Packet Supports

1. **Whether to extend the NCP schema** with transmission, provenance, linguistic frame, performativity, and semantic access layers
2. **How to structure those extensions** — as optional, additive, backward-compatible layers
3. **What governance model** to adopt for Indigenous knowledge in digital protocols (CARE, OCAP, TK Labels)
4. **What NOT to digitize** — where the protocol must express *limits* rather than *capabilities*
5. **How to sequence implementation** — which extensions are technically straightforward vs. which require community partnership before proceeding
