## Canonical Terms

| Term | Definition | Source Field |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Linguistic Relativity** | The principle that grammatical and categorical structure of a language shapes how its speakers perceive and organize reality. Operative weak version: language *constrains the default frame*. | Ethnolinguistics |
| **Semantic Shift** | When a concept migrates from one language into another, it loses or gains valence based on what the target language's grammar makes available. | Linguistic Anthropology |
| **Felicity Conditions** | Conditions that must be satisfied for a speech act (including storytelling) to succeed — right teller, right listener, right occasion, right form. | Speech Act Theory |
| **Performativity** | The property of an utterance or act that constitutes a social reality rather than merely describing one. Telling a story IS an act, not just a report. | Pragmatics / Butler |
| **Transmissible Knowledge** | Stories, songs, place names that circulate with some openness, though still contextually bounded. | Indigenous Epistemology |
| **Protocol-Gated Knowledge** | Knowledge that requires a relationship, ceremony, role, or life stage before it can be received. Access control at the semantic layer. | Indigenous Epistemology |
| **Enacted Knowledge** | Knowledge that cannot be extracted from its performance context. It exists as a *doing*, not a proposition. | Oral Tradition Studies |
| **Storywork** | Jo-Ann Archibald's framework treating stories as living entities with their own protocols (respect, responsibility, reciprocity, reverence, holism, interrelatedness, synergy). | Storywork Methodology |
| **OCAP** | Ownership, Control, Access, Possession — First Nations data governance principles. | Indigenous Data Sovereignty |
| **CARE Principles** | Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics — for Indigenous data governance. | GIDA |
| **TK Labels** | Traditional Knowledge Labels — metadata system expressing Indigenous expectations about digital content. | Local Contexts |
| **Relational Provenance** | Who is accountable for this knowledge, to whom, under what conditions, and with what obligations. Richer than bibliographic attribution. | Indigenous Epistemology |
| **Two-Eyed Seeing** | Mi'kmaw framework (Etuaptmumk) for holding Indigenous and Western knowledge systems together without collapsing either. | Albert Marshall |
| **Narrative Felicity** | Extension of Austin/Searle: conditions under which a story does its work — right procedure, teller, listener, occasion, form, intent. | Speech Act Theory + Storywork |

## MECE Field Decomposition

### Field 1: Linguistic Relativity & Ethnolinguistics
**Scope**: How language pre-structures what can be said about narrative. Sapir-Whorf, grammatical encoding (animacy, evidentiality, aspect), semantic shift in translation, computational/protocol implications.
**Boundary**: Does not cover validation workflows or story ethics.

### Field 2: Indigenous Epistemology & Research Methodology
**Scope**: How Indigenous knowledge systems validate, authorize, and transmit knowledge. Knowledge classification, community authorization, the four-stage validation workflow, OCAP/CARE, Two-Eyed Seeing.
**Boundary**: Does not cover linguistic theory or speech act foundations.

### Field 3: Speech Act Theory & Pragmatics
**Scope**: How stories function as performative acts with conditions. Austin, Searle, Grice, Butler, Bauman, Hymes. Felicity conditions, performativity, the SPEAKING model, pragmatics.
**Boundary**: Does not cover Indigenous methodology or protocol extensions.

### Field 4: Oral Tradition Studies & Storywork Methodology
**Scope**: How oral traditions encode, validate, and transmit narrative. Archibald's storywork, Ong's orality, mnemonic technologies, listener as participant, archiving vs. transmitting.
**Boundary**: Does not cover linguistic relativity or speech act foundations.

### Field 5: NCP Decolonization — Protocol Extensions
**Scope**: Decolonial critique of NCP's Dramatica/JSON assumptions. Five proposed schema extensions (transmission, provenance, linguistic_frame, performativity, semantic_access). Alignment with CARE/OCAP/TK Labels/UNDRIP. Anti-extraction safeguards.
**Boundary**: Does not cover implementation code.

## Source Quality Rules

1. **Prefer primary Indigenous scholarship** — cite Indigenous scholars first (Smith, Wilson, Kovach, Archibald, Cajete, Little Bear, Kimmerer)
2. **Prefer peer-reviewed** — academic papers, journals (AlterNative, International Indigenous Policy Journal), foundational texts
3. **Accept strong practitioner writing** — e.g., Mukurtu documentation, Local Contexts project, GIDA publications
4. **Flag training-knowledge-only sources** — mark as `verified: false` in source ledger
5. **No extractive citation** — do not quote or reproduce specific Indigenous stories, songs, or ceremonial content
6. **Respect publication conditions** — if a source was published with conditions (e.g., educational use only), note those conditions
